    Understanding transits

    Short introductions to the ideas behind the charts — written for reflection, not prediction.

    What is a transit?

    A transit is the current position of a planet measured against the sky at the moment of your birth. As the planets keep moving, they pass through the signs and houses of your birth chart, and astrologers read those passages as periods with a particular flavour.

    Fast planets — the Moon, Mercury, Venus — transit quickly, so their effects are read as passing moods and short episodes. Slow planets — Jupiter, Saturn, and beyond — spend months or years in one place, and their transits are read as longer chapters: growth, restructuring, or change.

    Mercury retrograde explained

    Three or four times a year, Mercury appears to move backwards through the zodiac for about three weeks. Nothing actually reverses — it is an apparent effect of Earth's own orbit — but the tradition reads it as a signal to slow down.

    Mercury rules communication, travel, and agreements, so a retrograde is treated as a good time to review, re-read, and revisit, and a poor time to assume a message was understood. The weeks just before and after — the shadow periods — are read the same way, more gently.

    Saturn return

    Saturn takes about 29.5 years to circle the zodiac, so it returns to the place it held at your birth around the ages of 29, 58, and 87. The first return is the most talked about, and the tradition reads it as a coming-of-age: a period of taking stock, committing, and building structures that last.

    It is often described as demanding rather than cruel — the questions it raises are about responsibility, direction, and what is worth keeping. The answers tend to shape the decade that follows.

    Jupiter transits

    Jupiter takes about 12 years to circle the zodiac, spending roughly a year in each sign. In the tradition it is the planet of expansion — growth, opportunity, learning, and confidence — so its transits are read as openings: the areas of life where things can grow if you meet them halfway.

    Because Jupiter moves slowly, its transits are read as themes for a year rather than events on a day. The sign it passes through colours the kind of growth on offer; the house it passes through in your chart shows where.

    Venus retrograde

    Venus turns retrograde roughly every 18 months, for about 40 days. Venus rules love, beauty, money, and what we value, so the tradition reads its retrograde as a time to review relationships and priorities rather than to start something new in those areas.

    Old connections and unfinished business have a habit of resurfacing. The usual advice in the tradition is to reflect before committing — and to be careful with big purchases and makeovers, which may look different once Venus turns direct again.

    Full Moon vs New Moon

    The New Moon, when the Moon sits between Earth and the Sun, is read as a beginning: a time to set intentions and start things quietly. The Full Moon, about two weeks later, when the Moon is fully lit, is read as a culmination: what was started comes into the open, and results — and feelings — are easier to see.

    The two weeks between them, the waxing half, are read as building; the two weeks after the Full Moon, the waning half, are read as release and reflection. The whole cycle repeats every 29.5 days.

      Questions & answers

      How are consultations answered?

      Each request is read alongside the asker's sign and birth details, when shared, and answered with a short written reading. Answers focus on themes, timing, and things to reflect on — not predictions set in stone.

      How does payment work?

      Pricing is confirmed with you personally before any payment. No payment is taken on this page — when you request a consultation, you will hear back with the price for the reading you chose before anything is charged.

      How long does an answer take?

      Requests are answered in the order they arrive, usually within a few days. Complex questions may take a little longer.

      Is my birth information kept private?

      In this standalone version, anything you enter stays in your own browser on your own device — it is not sent to a server. Only share what you're comfortable sharing.

      Can I ask about health, money, or legal matters?

      You can ask, but readings here are for entertainment and reflection only. They are not medical, financial, or legal advice. For decisions in those areas, please speak to a qualified professional.

      How is my zodiac sign decided?

      By your date of birth, using the tropical zodiac — the system most Western horoscopes use. If you were born near a sign changeover, your birth time and place can pin it down more precisely.
